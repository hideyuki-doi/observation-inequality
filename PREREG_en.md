## 1. Variable definitions

* All masses are wet mass in grams. Models use natural logarithms (the slope β does not depend on the base; results are also reported in log10).
* **R_g**: total GBIF records for group g, 2000–2024 (25 years), excluding fossil specimens.
* **Record type k**:
  * all: everything except fossils
  * human_obs: HUMAN_OBSERVATION without the DNA extension
  * specimen: PRESERVED_SPECIMEN
  * dna: records from datasets carrying the DNA-derived data extension
* **N_g, B_g, M_g = B_g/N_g**: main-analysis rows of census_main.csv (role = main, 10 groups).
* **r_g = R_g/N_g** is the primary response. Secondary responses are R_g/B_g (equivalent to slope β − 1) and R_g/S_g (S_g = number of described species).

## 2. Census uncertainty

Each value is converted to a normal error σ on the natural-log scale:

| Reported form | σ (ln) |
|---|---|
| 95% interval [lo, hi], or a range (treated as 95%) | (ln hi − ln lo)/(2 × 1.96) |
| 90% interval | (ln hi − ln lo)/(2 × 1.645) |
| fold f (Bar-On; "a range akin to a 95% CI") | ln f / 1.96 |
| mean ± SEM | SEM/mean |
| "±" of unknown meaning (van den Hoogen N) | the reported ± is treated as the 95% half-width, ln((x + d)/(x − d))/(2 × 1.96); if smaller than the default below, the default is used |
| no uncertainty reported | **default fold 10** (main); folds 5 and 20 in sensitivity analysis S7 |
| Bar-On subgroups' assumed individual mass m (no uncertainty) | **default fold 3** (main); fold 10 in S7 |

When a range is asymmetric (e.g. krill 5.4–8 × 10¹⁴ around a point estimate of 7.8 × 10¹⁴), only the width is used and the centre is the point estimate.

Census observations per group are two:
* Where N and B are reported independently (soil nematodes, springtails, mites, ants, krill): obs = {ln N̂, ln B̂}.
* Where N is Bar-On's "B ÷ assumed mass" (copepods, enchytraeids, pteropods, jellyfish, mesopelagic fish): obs = {ln B̂, ln m̂}. N then follows from B and m, and the model handles the error correlation automatically.

## 3. Tier B model (H1, H2, H4)

`models/tierB_eiv.stan` (errors-in-variables):

```
latent:   lnN_g, lnM_g
census:   ln N̂ ~ Normal(lnN_g, σ),  ln B̂ ~ Normal(lnN_g + lnM_g, σ),  ln m̂ ~ Normal(lnM_g, σ)
records:  ln R_gk ~ Normal(lnN_g + a_k + b_k (lnM_g − c),  sqrt(τ_k² + 1/R_gk))
          c = mean of observed ln M̂ (fixed from data); 1/R is the delta-method approximation of Poisson variance
```

Hence ln r_g = a + b (ln M_g − c) + ε, ε ~ Normal(0, τ); **β = b**.

**Priors:** lnN_g ~ N(40, 25), lnM_g ~ N(−8, 25), a ~ N(−25, 10), b ~ N(0, 2), τ ~ Half-Normal(0, 2).

**Fitting:** cmdstanr, 4 chains × (1,000 warm-up + 1,000 sampling), adapt_delta = 0.99, seed 20261103.

**Convergence criteria:**
* R-hat < 1.01 and bulk/tail ESS > 400; if not met, increase sampling to 4,000.
* If divergent transitions exceed 0.5%, also report a version with τ ~ Half-Normal(0, 1).

**Bayesian R²:** var_g(a + b(lnM_g − c)) / (the same + τ²); decisions use the posterior median.

**K (record types) are fitted jointly**, sharing latent N and M, with a_k, b_k, τ_k per type. Group × type cells with R_gk = 0 are excluded from that type's fit and reported. [Translator's note: Reviewer 3 showed that joint fitting double-counts the overlapping record types; see revision.]

**Parameter-recovery test** (simulated data, G = 10, fold-10 errors, `sim/test_tierB_recovery.R`, `models/recovery_test_tierB.csv`):
* True β ∈ {0.67, 1, 1.5} × τ ∈ {0.5, 2}: the 95% interval contained the true value in 11 of 12 runs.
* Interval widths were 0.29–0.93.

### Decision criteria

* **H1 (clean scaling):** for "all", width of the 95% CrI of β < 0.3 **and** posterior median Bayesian R² > 0.7.
  * As an auxiliary description, width < 0.6 and R² > 0.7 is written as "scaling present (limited precision)", because in the recovery test a width < 0.3 is rarely achievable with 10 groups and fold-10 errors.
  * If neither is met, report "scaling not confirmed" and stop at the Phase 4 gate.
* **H2:**
  * P(β − 2/3 > 0.2) > 0.95 → "human interest dominant"
  * P(β − 2/3 < −0.2) > 0.95 → "flatter than the optical prediction"
  * otherwise → "indistinguishable from the optical prediction"
* **H4:**
  * P(b_dna < b_human_obs) > 0.9 → supported
  * P(b_dna > b_human_obs) > 0.9 → reported as support in the opposite direction
  * otherwise → undecided
  * The main H4 analysis uses the 10 animal groups. A secondary analysis adds bacteria and archaea to dna only, reported separately because the M range changes.
  * Adding BOLD counts as a fifth type is a secondary analysis.

## 4. Tier A model (species level)

* **Mammals (main):** species with population reports in Greenspoon 2023 (392 species) that match GBIF and have a body mass.
  * When several species map to one accepted name (lumps), N is summed into one point, with body mass N-weighted.
  * The response R_s is records 2000–2024, zeros included.
* **Fit** (brms + cmdstanr): R_s ~ Poisson-lognormal, log λ_s = ln N_s + α + β ln M_s + (1 | order) + ε_s.
  * The error of ln N_s is taken from Greenspoon's 2.5–97.5% interval and entered with `me()`.
  * Priors: β ~ N(0, 2), α ~ N(−15, 10), SD ~ Half-Normal(0, 2).
* **Sensitivity:**
  * all 4,795 Greenspoon species (including SVR-predicted species, flagged allometry-derived)
  * birds (Callaghan, flagged occurrence-derived)
* **Use in decisions:** Tier A is not used for H1/H2. Its β and interval are reported, and "consistency" is described as whether its 95% interval overlaps Tier B's.
* **For Phase 5:** the negative-binomial overdispersion φ and year fluctuation are estimated from mammal species × year records, y_st ~ NegBin(μ_st, φ), log μ_st = species_s + year_t (2000–2024, report-based species).

## 5. Detection-delay simulation (H3, Phase 5)

**Generating model** (as in CLAUDE.md Phase 5):
* Decline scenario: N_s(t) = N_s0 exp(−d t), d = −ln(0.7)/10 (30% decline in 10 years). Control: d = 0.
* y_st ~ NegBin(r_s N_s(t) E_t, φ).
* E_t:
  * Years 1–25: the group's annual GBIF total (2000–2024), normalised to 2000 = 1.
  * Year 26 onward: held at the 2024 level (main); log-linear extrapolation of 2000–2024 growth as sensitivity.
* φ and year fluctuation: posterior medians from §4 (2.5% and 97.5% points as sensitivity).

**Analyst methods** (significance level 0.05, one-sided, decline direction):
* (a) NB-GLM with effort offset, one-sided test of the year slope.
* (b) Relative reporting rate: the species' records ÷ the group's total, regressed on year (binomial GLM, one-sided).
* (c) NB-GLM of raw counts (no offset).

**T_detect:** the first monitoring length T (1–100 years) with power ≥ 0.8; if not reached by 100 years, "undetectable". λ0 (initial expected records) takes 36 log-spaced values from 10⁻² to 10⁵, each with 1,000 replicates.

**Mapping T_detect onto body mass M:**
* Within-group species abundance distribution: log-normal, with the number of species = described species S_g and the mean from N_g/S_g; σ_log10 ∈ {1, 1.5, 2} (main 1.5).
* Each species' initial expected records: λ_s0 = r̂(M_g) × w_g × N_s.
  * r̂(M_g): predicted 25-year records per individual from the §3 model; uncertainty propagated with 1,000 posterior draws.
  * w_g = E_2024 / Σ_{t=2000}^{2024} E_t, converting the 25-year total to one 2024 year. [Translator's note: combining this 2024-scaled λ0 with an effort path starting in 2000 double-counts effort declines; disclosed at revision.]
  * Within-group detection rate assumed constant (sensitivity: species-level log-normal variation with SD = τ).

**H3 decision:** "size-structured detection delay" if the ratio of posterior median T_detect (method a, σ = 1.5) between the lower and upper thirds of the M range (log M range of the 10 main groups split into equal thirds) is ≥ 10.
* If the lower third contains a majority of "undetectable" species, the ratio also counts as ≥ 10.

**By-products reported:**
* Share of species undetectable within 25 years, by size class.
* Share of "significant increase" from method (c) in the control scenario (false increases).

## 6. Exclusion rules

1. The main analysis uses only method_class = independent rows (census_main.csv role = main).
2. Group × type cells with R_gk = 0 are excluded from that type's fit.
3. Tier A: species without body mass, not matched to GBIF, or with N = 0 are excluded; lumps are summed (§4).
4. Records lacking a year are not in the 2000–2024 totals (they are included in cumulative sensitivity S1).

## 8. Interpretive cautions (written before seeing results)

* Tier B has 10 groups with M spanning about 7 orders of magnitude (5 × 10⁻⁷ to 3 g); the only vertebrates are mesopelagic fishes. Conclusions about large organisms depend on Tier A (mammals).
* R counts records, not individuals. Plankton tend to give one record per sample, biasing r downward.
* Nematodes (all Nematoda): R includes out-of-scope nematodes, biasing r upward.

## Change log

(appended after finalisation)

### 2026-11-03 Finalisation (v1.0)
* Approved by Doi at the Phase 3 gate ("all OK, proceed").
* The H1 threshold of 0.3 retained, with the §3 auxiliary description (width < 0.6 and R² > 0.7).
* H2 two-sided decisions, H3 settings and the role of Tier A as drafted.

### 2026-11-03 Implementation change (§4, Tier A)
* **Problem:** the brms model with `me()` and an observation-level random effect hit maximum tree depth in 99–100% of transitions, with E-BFMI < 0.3, for mammals; for birds it failed outright.
* **Cause:** each species has a single observation (R_s), so only the sum of the ln N measurement error and the overdispersion ε is identified, producing a ridge.
* **Fix:** both are independent normals entering the linear predictor additively, so marginalising them into one normal term with SD √(sdN² + τ²) gives an equivalent model, written directly in Stan (`models/tierA_pln.stan`). Priors, response, predictors and exclusion rules are unchanged; brms is no longer used.
* **Addendum (same day):** the marginalised version with non-centred z still did not converge (R-hat ≈ 4, all transitions at maximum tree depth). Because R strongly determines η for well-recorded species, η was switched to a centred parameterisation, which converged (R-hat 1.00, 0 divergences). The model is the same.

### 2026-11-03 Implementation change (§5, H3 simulation)
* T_detect found by bisection assuming power increases with T (about 8 evaluations) instead of evaluating all T = 1–100, which would require about 10⁸ NB-GLM fits. Power at each T is cached and shared among the three methods.
* The lower bound of T is 3, since a slope test needs at least three years.
* The denominator of method (b) is "the species' records + other species' records O_t", with O_t the group's actual total record trajectory (2000–2024, then held at the 2024 level).
* Series whose NB-GLM fit fails are counted as "not detected" (conservative), and the failure rate is recorded.
* φ was estimated from report-based mammal species × year (brms, fixed effects species + year): φ = 0.69 [0.66, 0.71], 382 species, 9,550 observations.

### 2026-11-03 Addendum on H3 interpretation (Phase 5 gate, approved by Doi)
* The H3 rule (a majority of undetectable species in the lower third counts as ≥ 10×) did not anticipate the case where every third is undetectable (100% within 25 years) in the main range.
* It is reported that the rule is formally met but "size-structured detection delay" was not demonstrated. The main finding is the overdispersion floor on detection (about 44 years under method a), and that below λ0 of about 0.04–0.4 records per year declines are undetectable even in 100 years.
* The following analyses are all labelled **exploratory (outside the preregistration)**:
  * census-free empirical version (observed species-level λ)
  * mapping without the SAD for single-species groups
  * φ estimated in invertebrates
  * 50% decline per 10 years
  * detection time for multi-species indicators

### 2026-11-03 Additional exploratory analyses (after Phase 7, at Doi's request)
* Independent vertebrate censuses, occupancy-based comparison, invertebrates' own φ, and the literature check. All outside the preregistration and marked [E] in reports; contents in `reports/phase7b.md`.
